1Forward discounted stochastic control

Throughout, $E=\R^d$ is the state space and $A\s\R^m$ is the set of actions, assumed compact. We write $n=d+m$, so that $E\times A\s\R^n$, and fix a discount rate $\rho>0$ and an initial law $\nu_0\in\mathscr P(E)$. The letter $\Omega$ is reserved for the sample space of a filtered probability space $(\Omega,\mathscr F,\{\mathscr F_t\},\P)$, and $W$ is a Brownian motion in $\R^d$.

The model is the controlled diffusion

\begin{equation} dX_t=b(X_t,a_t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t,a_t)\,dW_t,\qquad X_0\sim\nu_0, \qquad \end{equation}

\begin{equation} \gen{a}\varphi(x)=b(x,a)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x) +\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\sigma\sigma^\top(x,a)\,D^2\varphi(x)\bigr). \qquad \end{equation}

We write $\gen{}$ for the operator with $\dom(\gen{})=C_0^2(\R^d)$ and range in $C_b(E\times A)$ given by

\begin{equation} \gen{}\varphi:(x,a)\longmapsto\gen{a}\varphi(x). \end{equation}

Supuesto 1.1

[Bhatt–Borkar, Ex. 1 and Cond. 1–3] The data $E$, $A$, $b$ and $\sigma$ satisfy the standing hypotheses of the source. In particular, for every $a\in A$ and every $x\in E$ the martingale problem for $(\gen{a},\delta_x)$ has a càdlàg solution.

Equation (1) presupposes an action process, and the linear formulation below needs to randomize it.

Definición 1.2

[Bhatt–Borkar, Def. 1.2] Let $V=\mathscr P(A)$, compact and metrizable in the weak topology. A process $(X,\pi)$ with values in $E\times V$, progressively measurable with respect to $\{\mathscr F_t\}$ and with $X_0\sim\nu_0$, solves the relaxed controlled martingale problem for $(\gen{},\nu_0)$ if for every $f\in\dom(\gen{})$ the process

\[f(X_t)-\int_0^t\!\!\int_A \gen{a}f(X_s)\,\pi_s(da)\,ds\]\[f(X_t)-\int_0^t\pi_s^a\,\gen{a}f(X_s)\,ds\]

\begin{equation} \Gen{\pi}\varphi(x) =\int_A\gen{a}\varphi(x)\,\pi_x(da) =b_\pi(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x) +\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Sigma_\pi(x)D^2\varphi(x)\bigr), \qquad \end{equation}

\[J_c(\pi)=\E\left[\int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}\int_A c(X_t,a)\,\pi_t(da)\,dt\right], \qquad \inf_\pi J_c(\pi),\qquad \]\[J_c(\pi)=\P\bigl(\lambda_\rho^t\,\pi_t^a\,c(X_t,a)\bigr), \qquad \inf_\pi J_c(\pi),\qquad \]

1.1Discounted occupation measures

Definición 1.3

[Bhatt–Borkar, (1.6)] For a relaxed policy $\pi$ and $B\in\mathscr B(E\times A)$, the discounted occupation measure is

\[\eta_\pi(B)=\E\left[\int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}\int_A \mathbf 1_B(X_t,a)\,\pi_t(da)\,dt\right].\qquad \]\[\eta_\pi(B)=\P\bigl(\lambda_\rho^t\,\pi_t^a\,\mathbf 1_B(X_t,a)\bigr). \qquad \]
v2: (9)(7)

Putting $c$ in place of $\mathbf 1_B$ in (7) and comparing with (6),

\[J_c(\pi)=\int_{E\times A}c\,d\eta_\pi=\pair{c}{\eta_\pi}.\qquad \]\[J_c(\pi)=\eta_\pi(c).\qquad \]
v2: (10)(8)

The right-hand side carries no time and no initial condition, so the forward problem, nonconvex in $\pi$, is linear in $\eta$. The measures that arise this way are characterized by a single family of linear equalities.

Proposición 1.4

[Bhatt–Borkar, (2.13) and Thm. 2.2] For every $\varphi\in\dom(\gen{})$, the occupation measure of any solution of the relaxed controlled martingale problem satisfies the weak balance identity

\[\int_{E\times A}\bigl(\rho\varphi(x)-\gen{a}\varphi(x)\bigr)\,\eta(dx,da) =\int_E\varphi\,d\nu_0.\qquad \]\[\eta\bigl(\rho\varphi-\gen{}\varphi\bigr)=\nu_0(\varphi).\qquad \]
v2: (11)(9)

Conversely, every nonnegative $\eta$ of total mass $1/\rho$ satisfying (9) is the occupation measure of some solution.

Taking expectations in the martingale of Definition 1.2 between $0$ and $t$,

\[\E[\varphi(X_t)]-\int_E\varphi\,d\nu_0 =\E\Bigl[\int_0^t\!\!\int_A\gen{a}\varphi(X_s)\,\pi_s(da)\,ds\Bigr] \qquad\text{for every }t\ge0 .\]

Multiply by $\rho e^{-\rho t}$ and integrate over $t\in(0,\infty)$. Both integrands are bounded, by $\|\varphi\|_\infty$ and $\|\gen{}\varphi\|_\infty$, and $\rho e^{-\rho t}dt$ is a probability measure, so Fubini applies. By Definition 1.3 the left-hand side becomes $\rho\int\varphi\,d\eta-\int\varphi\,d\nu_0$, and on the right, interchanging $s$ and $t$,

\[\int_0^\infty\!\!\rho e^{-\rho t}\!\int_0^t(\cdot)\,ds\,dt =\int_0^\infty\!(\cdot)\int_s^\infty\!\!\rho e^{-\rho t}dt\,ds =\int_0^\infty\!\!e^{-\rho s}(\cdot)\,ds ,\]

which is $\int\gen{}\varphi\,d\eta$. Equating the two sides gives (9). The converse is [Bhatt–Borkar, Thm. 2.2] applied to the probability measure $\rho\,\eta$.

Corolario 1.5

$\eta_\pi(E\times A)=1/\rho$ for every relaxed policy $\pi$.

Take $B=E\times A$ in Definition 1.3: the inner integral equals $\pi_t(A)=1$, and $\eta_\pi(E\times A)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}dt=1/\rho$.

Let $\M$ denote the set of nonnegative measures on $E\times A$ of mass $1/\rho$ satisfying (9).

2Inverse control with a prior cost

We observe an expert and ask which running cost explains it. The expert is a policy $\pi_E$, known only through its discounted occupation measure $\eta_E\in\M$, and a prior running cost $\ch$ is given. Requiring the expert to be exactly optimal is too rigid, and the relaxation we use measures how far it is from optimal.

Definición 2.1

The suboptimality gap of the expert under a candidate cost $c$ is

\[\Gap(c):=\pair{c}{\eta_E}-\inf_{\eta\in\M}\pair{c}{\eta} =\sup_{\eta\in\M}\pair{c}{\eta_E-\eta}.\qquad \]\[\Gap(c):=\eta_E(c)-\inf_{\eta\in\M}\eta(c) =\sup_{\eta\in\M}\,(\eta_E-\eta)(c).\qquad \]
v2: (14)(10)
Proposición 2.2

$\Gap$ is convex, positively homogeneous and nonnegative on $\Hs$, with $\Gap(0)=0$, and $\Gap(c)=0$ if and only if $\eta_E$ minimizes $\pair{c}{\cdot}$ over $\M$.

For each $\eta\in\M$ the map $c\mapsto\pair{c}{\eta_E-\eta}$ is linear, and a pointwise supremum of linear maps is convex and positively homogeneous. Taking $\eta=\eta_E$, admissible since $\eta_E\in\M$, gives $\Gap(c)\ge0$, and the same choice gives $\Gap(0)=0$. Finally $\Gap(c)=0$ means $\pair{c}{\eta_E}\le\pair{c}{\eta}$ for all $\eta\in\M$, which is optimality of $\eta_E$ for $c$.

Since $\Gap(0)=0$, the gap alone does not select a cost. The prior does, through a penalty on the distance to it:

\begin{equation} \min_{c\in\Hs}\ \left\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\|c-\ch\|_{\Hs}^2+\Gap(c)\right\}, \qquad\alpha>0, \qquad \end{equation}
v2: (15)(11)

or, expanding the gap,

\[\min_{c\in\Hs}\ \max_{\eta\in\M}\ \left\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\|c-\ch\|_{\Hs}^2+\pair{c}{\eta_E-\eta}\right\}. \qquad \]\[\min_{c\in\Hs}\ \max_{\eta\in\M}\ \left\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\|c-\ch\|_{\Hs}^2+(\eta_E-\eta)(c)\right\}. \qquad \]
v2: (16)(12)

Large $\alpha$ pins the recovered cost to the prior; small $\alpha$ gives priority to closing the expert's gap.

2.1The cost space

For (10) to make sense, the pairing $\pair{c}{\eta}$ must be defined for every $c\in\Hs$ and every $\eta\in\M$; that is, we need $\M\s\Hd$. We write $\widehat c(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\R^n}c(z)\,e^{-iz\cdot\xi}\,dz$ for the unitary Fourier transform.

Definición 2.3

Let $s>0$ and $\kappa>0$. The cost space is $\Hs=H^s(\R^n)$ with

\begin{equation} \|c\|_{\Hs}^2=\int_{\R^n}\bigl(1+\kappa|\xi|^2\bigr)^{s}\,|\widehat c(\xi)|^2\,d\xi =\int_{\R^n}c\,(\mathrm{Id}-\kappa\Delta)^s c , \qquad \end{equation}
v2: (17)(13)

the second expression being valid for smooth $c$ when $s$ is an integer.

For $s=1$ the norm is $\int(|c|^2+\kappa|\nabla c|^2)$, which is the choice of the note. Costs are defined on $\R^n$ and paired with measures on $E\times A$.

Proposición 2.4

The Riesz isomorphism $R:\Hs\to\Hd$ is

\begin{equation} R=(\mathrm{Id}-\kappa\Delta)^{s}, \qquad \text{with symbol}\quad\bigl(1+\kappa|\xi|^2\bigr)^{s}, \end{equation}
(14)

an operator of order $2s$ on $\R^n$, with no boundary conditions.

By definition $Rc$ is the functional $v\mapsto(c,v)_{\Hs}$, which by Plancherel and (13) is $v\mapsto\int(1+\kappa|\xi|^2)^s\,\widehat c(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat v(\xi)}\,d\xi$. With the unitary transform, $-\Delta$ has symbol $|\xi|^2$, so $R$ is the Fourier multiplier $(1+\kappa|\xi|^2)^s$, that is $(\mathrm{Id}-\kappa\Delta)^s$. It is an isomorphism by the Riesz representation theorem, its symbol grows like $\kappa^s|\xi|^{2s}$, and since it acts on all of $\R^n$ there is no boundary term.

The inclusion $\M\s\Hd$ holds for every finite measure once $s$ exceeds a Sobolev threshold.

Teorema 2.5

[Folland, Thm. 9.17] Suppose $s>n/2$. Then $H^s(\R^n)\hookrightarrow C_0(\R^n)$ continuously, and every $f\in H^s$ has a continuous representative with $\|f\|_\infty\le C_\iota\|f\|_{\Hs}$, where $C_\iota$ depends only on $n$, $s$ and $\kappa$.

Proposición 2.6

Suppose $s>n/2$. Then $\delta_z\in\Hd$ with $\|\delta_z\|_{\Hd}\le C_\iota$ for every $z\in\R^n$, and every finite signed measure $\eta$ on $E\times A$ belongs to $\Hd$ with $\|\eta\|_{\Hd}\le C_\iota\,|\eta|(E\times A)$.

By Theorem 2.5, $|\delta_z(c)|=|c(z)|\le\|c\|_\infty\le C_\iota\|c\|_{\Hs}$ for every $z$, so $\|\delta_z\|_{\Hd}\le C_\iota$. For a finite signed measure $\eta$ on $E\times A$,

\[|\pair{c}{\eta}|\le\|c\|_\infty\,|\eta|(E\times A) \le C_\iota\,|\eta|(E\times A)\,\|c\|_{\Hs},\]

and the supremum over the unit ball of $\Hs$ gives the bound.

3Convex structure, existence, and the reduced problem

In this section $s>n/2$, so that $\Hs\hookrightarrow C(E\times A)$ with constant $C_\iota$ by Theorem 2.5. We write $\Phi$ for the saddle functional:

\begin{equation} \Phi(c,\eta)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\|c-\ch\|_{\Hs}^2+\pair{c}{\eta_E-\eta}, \qquad \inf_{c\in\Hs}\ \sup_{\eta\in\M}\ \Phi(c,\eta). \qquad \end{equation}
v2: (18)(15)
Lema 3.1

Every $\eta\in\M$ satisfies $\|\eta\|_{\Hd}\le C_\iota/\rho$, and $\operatorname{diam}_{\Hd}(\M)\le 2C_\iota/\rho$.

Let $\eta\in\M$. By Corollary 1.5 it is nonnegative with $\eta(E\times A)=1/\rho$, so Proposition 2.6 gives $\|\eta\|_{\Hd}\le C_\iota/\rho$. The diameter bound is the triangle inequality.

Corolario 3.2

$\Gap$ is finite on $\Hs$ and Lipschitz with constant $2C_\iota/\rho$.

From $|\sup_if_i-\sup_ig_i|\le\sup_i|f_i-g_i|$ applied to (10),

\[|\Gap(c)-\Gap(c')|\le\sup_{\eta\in\M}|\pair{c-c'}{\eta_E-\eta}| \le\|c-c'\|_{\Hs}\sup_{\eta\in\M}\|\eta_E-\eta\|_{\Hd} \le\frac{2C_\iota}{\rho}\|c-c'\|_{\Hs}\]

by Lemma 3.1. Finiteness follows by taking $c'=0$, where $\Gap(0)=0$.

Proposición 3.3

Suppose $\M\ne\emptyset$. Then the objective in (11) is $\alpha$-strongly convex, coercive and lower semicontinuous, and it has a unique minimizer $c_\star\in\Hs$.

The squared Hilbert norm is $\alpha$-strongly convex and $\Gap$ is convex by Proposition 2.2, so the objective is $\alpha$-strongly convex and has at most one minimizer. Since $\Gap\ge0$, the objective dominates $\frac{\alpha}{2}\|c-\ch\|_{\Hs}^2$ and is coercive, and Corollary 3.2 gives lower semicontinuity. The direct method gives existence.

No property of the map from costs to optimal policies is used.

3.1Eliminating the cost without assuming a minimax interchange

For fixed $\eta$ the minimization in $c$ is explicit.

Proposición 3.4

\begin{equation} c_\eta=\ch+\frac1\alpha R^{-1}(\eta-\eta_E), \qquad \end{equation}

\[\inf_{c\in\Hs}\Phi(c,\eta)=-\Psi(\eta),\qquad \Psi(\eta):=\pair{\ch}{\eta-\eta_E} +\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta-\eta_E\|_{\Hd}^2.\qquad \]\[\inf_{c\in\Hs}\Phi(c,\eta)=-\Psi(\eta),\qquad \Psi(\eta):=(\eta-\eta_E)(\ch) +\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta-\eta_E\|_{\Hd}^2.\qquad \]

Proposición 3.5

Suppose $\rho\,\M$ is tight and $\gen{}$ maps $\dom(\gen{})$ into $C(E\times A)$. Then $\M$ is convex and weakly compact in $\Hd$.

Convexity holds because (9) is a family of linear equalities and $\eta\ge0$ is a convex constraint. Put $\widehat\M=\rho\,\M\s\mathscr P(E\times A)$. Tightness and Prokhorov's theorem make $\widehat\M$ relatively weak-$*$ compact, and it is weak-$*$ closed because each constraint $\mu\mapsto\int(\rho\varphi-\gen{}\varphi)\,d\mu-\rho\int_E\varphi\,d\nu_0$ is weak-$*$ continuous, the test function $\rho\varphi-\gen{}\varphi$ lying in $C(E\times A)$ by hypothesis. Hence $\widehat\M$ is weak-$*$ compact.

Let $\iota:\Hs\to C(E\times A)$ be the embedding. For fixed $c$ the map $\mu\mapsto\pair{\iota^{*}\mu}{c}=\int\iota c\,d\mu$ is weak-$*$ continuous, so $\iota^{*}$ is continuous from the weak-$*$ topology to $\sigma(\Hd,\Hs)$, which is the weak topology of the Hilbert space $\Hd$. Therefore $\M=\rho^{-1}\iota^{*}(\widehat\M)$ is weakly compact.

\begin{equation} \Phi(c_\star,\eta)\ \le\ \Phi(c_\star,\eta_{A,\star})\ \le\ \Phi(c,\eta_{A,\star}) \qquad\text{for all }c\in\Hs,\ \eta\in\M, \qquad \end{equation}

\begin{equation} \inf_{c\in\Hs}\sup_{\eta\in\M}\Phi =\Phi(c_\star,\eta_{A,\star}) =\sup_{\eta\in\M}\inf_{c\in\Hs}\Phi, \qquad \end{equation}

4The optimality system

Theorem 3.6 identifies the solution as a pair $(c_\star,\eta_{A,\star})$ characterized by a variational inequality. We now write that characterization as a system of equations. Nothing new is assumed about the inverse problem; what is assumed is regularity of the occupation measures, so that the balance identity can be read as a partial differential equation.

4.1From weak balance to Fokker–Planck

Throughout this section $\pi$ is a relaxed Markov control and the occupation measure disintegrates as

\begin{equation} \eta_\pi(dx,da)=m_\pi(x)\,\pi_x(da)\,dx, \end{equation}
(22)

with $m_\pi\in L^1(E)$ the discounted state density.

Proposición 4.1

Suppose $\eta_\pi\in\M$ disintegrates as (22). Then, for every $\varphi\in\dom(\gen{})$,

\[\int_E\bigl(\rho\varphi-\Gen{\pi}\varphi\bigr)\,m_\pi\,dx =\int_E\varphi\,d\nu_0,\]\[(m_\pi\,dx)\bigl(\rho\varphi-\Gen{\pi}\varphi\bigr)=\nu_0(\varphi),\]
(23)

that is, $(\rho\,\mathrm{Id}-\Gen{\pi})^{*}m_\pi=\nu_0$ in the sense of distributions. If moreover $b_\pi$ and $\Sigma_\pi$ are smooth enough for the adjoint to be computed classically, then

\begin{equation} \rho\,m_\pi+\nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(b_\pi m_\pi\bigr) -\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\partial^2_{x_ix_j}\bigl(\Sigma_{\pi,ij}\,m_\pi\bigr)=\nu_0 . \qquad \end{equation}
v2: (36)(24)

Take $\varphi\in\dom(\gen{})$, a function of $x$ alone, in (9). By (22), integrating out the action first,

\[\int\bigl(\rho\varphi-\gen{}\varphi\bigr)d\eta_\pi =\int_E\Bigl(\rho\varphi(x)-\!\int_A\!\gen{a}\varphi(x)\,\pi_x(da)\Bigr)m_\pi(x)\,dx =\int_E\bigl(\rho\varphi-\Gen{\pi}\varphi\bigr)m_\pi\,dx,\]

where the last step is (5). Equating with $\int_E\varphi\,d\nu_0$ gives (23). For the classical form, integrate by parts in $\int(b_\pi\cdot\nabla\varphi)m_\pi$ and $\tfrac12\int\operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma_\pi D^2\varphi)m_\pi$ to move all derivatives onto $m_\pi$; there are no boundary terms, since $\varphi$ and its first two derivatives vanish at infinity.

Equation (24) is the stationary Fokker–Planck equation with discount $\rho$ and source $\nu_0$; by Corollary 1.5 its solution has total mass $1/\rho$.

4.2The linear program and its dual

By (8) and Proposition 1.4, for a fixed cost $c$ the forward problem is the linear program

\[\inf_{\eta\in\M}\ \pair{c}{\eta},\qquad \]\[\inf_{\eta\in\M}\ \eta(c),\qquad \]

\begin{equation} \sup_V\ \int_E V\,d\nu_0 \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad c(x,a)+\gen{a}V(x)-\rho V(x)\ \ge\ 0 . \qquad \end{equation}

The constraint in (26) is the HJB inequality, and the value function of the forward problem is the dual variable attached to the balance constraint.

4.3The coupled system

Let $\pi_E$ be the expert policy with state density $m_E$, let $\pi_A$ be an optimal policy for the recovered cost $c$ with state density $m_A$, and let $V$ be the corresponding dual variable in (26). Complementary slackness between (25) and (26) turns the constraint into an equality on the support of $\eta_A$, and together with Proposition 4.1 and (19) this gives the optimality system

\begin{equation} \left\{ \begin{aligned} \rho V(x)&=\inf_{a\in A}\bigl\{c(x,a)+\gen{a}V(x)\bigr\}, \\[1mm] \operatorname{supp}\pi_{A,x}&\s\arg\min_{a\in A} \bigl\{c(x,a)+\gen{a}V(x)\bigr\}, \\[1mm] (\rho\,\mathrm{Id}-\Gen{\pi_A})^{*}m_A&=\nu_0, \\[1mm] (\rho\,\mathrm{Id}-\Gen{\pi_E})^{*}m_E&=\nu_0, \\[1mm] \alpha\,R\,(c-\ch)&=\eta_A-\eta_E, \end{aligned} \right. \qquad \end{equation}

where $\eta_A=m_A\pi_A$ and $\eta_E=m_E\pi_E$ in the sense of (22). The last equation is read weakly:

\[\alpha\,(c-\ch,h)_{\Hs}=\int_{E\times A}h\,d(\eta_A-\eta_E) \qquad\text{for all }h\in\Hs,\qquad \]\[\alpha\,(c-\ch,h)_{\Hs}=(\eta_A-\eta_E)(h) \qquad\text{for all }h\in\Hs,\qquad \]

with the inner product of Definition 2.3, which is meaningful whenever $\eta_A-\eta_E\in\Hd$.

Proposición 4.2

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem 3.6, and that the occupation measures of $\pi_E$ and of some optimal $\pi_A$ disintegrate as (22). Then $(c_\star,\eta_{A,\star})$ solves (27) in the weak sense. Conversely, every weak solution of (27) with $\eta_A\in\M$ has $c=c_\star$.

By Theorem 3.6, $\eta_{A,\star}$ minimizes $\pair{c_\star}{\cdot}$ over $\M$, which by (25) and (26) amounts to the existence of $V$ with $c_\star(x,a)+\gen{a}V(x)-\rho V(x)\ge0$ on $E\times A$ and equality on $\operatorname{supp}\eta_{A,\star}$. Since the inequality holds for every $a$, equality on $\operatorname{supp}\pi_{A,x}$ says that the infimum over $a$ is attained there and equals $\rho V(x)$; these are the first two blocks. The two adjoint equations are Proposition 4.1 applied to $\pi_A$ and to $\pi_E$. The last block is (19) rearranged, and its weak form is (28).

Conversely, let $(V,\pi_A,m_A,m_E,c)$ solve (27) weakly with $\eta_A\in\M$. The last block gives $c=\ch+\alpha^{-1}R^{-1}(\eta_A-\eta_E)$, and the first two give optimality of $\eta_A$ for $c$, so $\pair{c}{\eta-\eta_A}\ge0$ for all $\eta\in\M$. This is the variational inequality characterizing the minimizer of $\Psi$, which is unique; hence $\eta_A=\eta_{A,\star}$ and $c=c_\star$.

The first two blocks determine the apprentice from the current cost and constitute the forward problem. The two adjoint equations transport the expert and apprentice occupations and are linear in $m$ once the policy is fixed. The last one deforms the cost away from the prior in proportion to the occupation mismatch, and is linear in $c$ once the measures are fixed. The only nonlinearity is the $\arg\min$.

4.4The system in measures

The disintegration (22) is needed to write (27) with densities, but not to characterize the solution. In terms of measures, the optimality conditions for a cost $c$, a measure $\eta$ and a dual variable $V$ are

\begin{equation} \left\{ \begin{aligned} &\eta\in\M, \\[1mm] &c(x,a)+\gen{a}V(x)-\rho V(x)\ \ge\ 0 \qquad\text{for all }(x,a)\in E\times A, \\[1mm] &\int_{E\times A}\bigl(c+\gen{}V-\rho V\bigr)\,d\eta=0, \\[1mm] &\alpha\,R\,(c-\ch)=\eta-\eta_E . \end{aligned} \right. \end{equation}
(29)

The third condition says that $\eta$ is concentrated on the set where the nonnegative function $c+\gen{}V-\rho V$ vanishes, and the last one is (16).

Proposición 4.3

Let $s>n/2$ and assume the hypotheses of Theorem 3.6. If $c\in\Hs$, $\eta\in\M$ and $V\in\dom(\gen{})$ satisfy (29), then $c=c_\star$ and $\eta=\eta_{A,\star}$. Conversely, $(c_\star,\eta_{A,\star})$ satisfies (29) with some $V\in\dom(\gen{})$ whenever the supremum in (26) for the cost $c_\star$ is attained in $\dom(\gen{})$ and equals the infimum in (25).

Let $\eta'\in\M$. Since $V\in\dom(\gen{})$, the balance identity (9) holds with $\varphi=V$ for $\eta'$ and for $\eta$, so

\[\pair{c}{\eta'}=\int\bigl(c+\gen{}V-\rho V\bigr)\,d\eta'+\int_E V\,d\nu_0 \ \ge\ \int_E V\,d\nu_0=\pair{c}{\eta},\]

by the second and third conditions in (29). Hence $\eta$ minimizes $\pair{c}{\cdot}$ over $\M$. The last condition gives $c=\ch+\alpha^{-1}R^{-1}(\eta-\eta_E)$, and the argument of the converse in Proposition 4.2 gives $\eta=\eta_{A,\star}$ and $c=c_\star$.

Conversely, $\eta_{A,\star}\in\M$, and the last condition is (19) rearranged. Let $V\in\dom(\gen{})$ attain the supremum in (26) for $c_\star$, with value equal to the infimum in (25), which is $\pair{c_\star}{\eta_{A,\star}}$ by Theorem 3.6. The constraint in (26) is the second condition, and by (9)

\[\int\bigl(c_\star+\gen{}V-\rho V\bigr)\,d\eta_{A,\star} =\pair{c_\star}{\eta_{A,\star}}-\int_E V\,d\nu_0=0 .\]

System (27) is the form that (29) takes when the occupation measures have densities. If $\eta=\eta_A$ disintegrates as (22), the first two rows of (27) give the second and third conditions of (29); conversely, these two conditions give back the first two rows of (27) for almost every $x$ with $m_A(x)>0$. The adjoint equations hold for every $\eta\in\M$ of that form by Proposition 4.1, and the last row is common to both systems.

5The reduced problem by Frank–Wolfe

Throughout this section $s>n/2$ and the hypotheses of Theorem 3.6 hold. The reduced problem (18) minimizes a convex function over the convex set $\M$, and minimizing a linear function over $\M$ is the forward problem (25). This is the setting of the Frank–Wolfe method [Frank–Wolfe], which needs the gradient of $\Psi$ and a solver for (25), and nothing else. For $\eta\in\Hd$ we write $c_\eta$ for the right-hand side of (16).

Lema 5.1

For all $\eta,\eta'\in\Hd$,

\begin{equation} \Psi(\eta')=\Psi(\eta)+\pair{c_\eta}{\eta'-\eta} +\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta'-\eta\|_{\Hd}^2 . \end{equation}
(30)

In particular $\Psi$ is Fr\'echet differentiable on $\Hd$ and, identifying the dual of $\Hd$ with $\Hs$, $\nabla\Psi(\eta)=c_\eta$.

By the Riesz isomorphism, $(\mu,\nu)_{\Hd}=\pair{R^{-1}\mu}{\nu}$ for $\mu,\nu\in\Hd$. Expanding the square around $\eta$,

\[\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta'-\eta_E\|_{\Hd}^2 =\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta-\eta_E\|_{\Hd}^2 +\frac1\alpha\pair{R^{-1}(\eta-\eta_E)}{\eta'-\eta} +\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta'-\eta\|_{\Hd}^2,\]

and adding $\pair{\ch}{\eta'-\eta_E}=\pair{\ch}{\eta-\eta_E}+\pair{\ch}{\eta'-\eta}$ gives (30) by (16). The map $\eta'\mapsto\pair{c_\eta}{\eta'}$ is continuous on $\Hd$ and the last term in (30) is $o(\|\eta'-\eta\|_{\Hd})$, which is Fr\'echet differentiability with derivative $c_\eta$.

Starting from $\eta_0\in\M$, the Frank–Wolfe method for (18) computes, for $k=0,1,2,\dots$,

\begin{equation} c_k=c_{\eta_k},\qquad \widetilde\eta_k\in\arg\min_{\eta\in\M}\pair{c_k}{\eta},\qquad \eta_{k+1}=(1-\tau_k)\,\eta_k+\tau_k\,\widetilde\eta_k, \end{equation}
(31)

with a step $\tau_k\in[0,1]$. The minimum exists because $\M$ is weakly compact and $\eta\mapsto\pair{c_k}{\eta}$ is weakly continuous, and $\eta_{k+1}\in\M$ because $\M$ is convex, so no projection onto $\M$ is needed. By Lemma 5.1, $c_k$ is the gradient of $\Psi$ at $\eta_k$, and by (8) and Proposition 1.4, $\widetilde\eta_k$ is the occupation measure of a control that is optimal for the cost $c_k$. The map $c\mapsto\widetilde\eta$ is the linear minimization oracle of the method.

In terms of (27), one step of (31) is one pass through its rows. With $c=c_k$ fixed, the first three rows determine an optimal policy and its occupation measure, which is the oracle. The fourth row does not change, since $\eta_E$ is data. The last row, solved for $c$ with $\eta_A=\eta_{k+1}$, gives $c_{k+1}$, and solving it is inverting the Riesz isomorphism. The relaxation with $\tau_k$ is the step of the method, and it is taken on the measures.

The method also provides a certificate that can be computed without knowing $\min\Psi$. For $\eta\in\M$, the Frank–Wolfe gap is

\begin{equation} g(\eta)=\pair{c_\eta}{\eta}-\min_{\eta'\in\M}\pair{c_\eta}{\eta'} =\max_{\eta'\in\M}\pair{c_\eta}{\eta-\eta'}, \end{equation}
(32)

and at the iterates $g(\eta_k)=\pair{c_k}{\eta_k-\widetilde\eta_k}$ is a by-product of the oracle.

Proposición 5.2

For every $\eta\in\M$,

\begin{equation} \frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta-\eta_{A,\star}\|_{\Hd}^2 \ \le\ \Psi(\eta)-\Psi(\eta_{A,\star})\ \le\ g(\eta), \qquad \|c_\eta-c_\star\|_{\Hs}\le\sqrt{2g(\eta)/\alpha}\, . \end{equation}
(33)

In particular $g(\eta)=0$ if and only if $\eta=\eta_{A,\star}$.

Write $\eta_\star=\eta_{A,\star}$. By (30) with $\eta'=\eta_\star$, and since $\eta_\star\in\M$,

\[\Psi(\eta_\star)\ \ge\ \Psi(\eta)+\pair{c_\eta}{\eta_\star-\eta} \ \ge\ \Psi(\eta)-g(\eta),\]

which is the right inequality. By (30) with the roles of $\eta$ and $\eta_\star$ exchanged, and since $c_{\eta_\star}=c_\star$ by (19),

\[\Psi(\eta)=\Psi(\eta_\star)+\pair{c_\star}{\eta-\eta_\star} +\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta-\eta_\star\|_{\Hd}^2 \ \ge\ \Psi(\eta_\star)+\frac1{2\alpha}\|\eta-\eta_\star\|_{\Hd}^2,\]

because $\eta_\star$ minimizes $\pair{c_\star}{\cdot}$ over $\M$ by Theorem 3.6. This is the left inequality. Finally $c_\eta-c_\star=\alpha^{-1}R^{-1}(\eta-\eta_\star)$ and $R$ is an isometry, so $\|c_\eta-c_\star\|_{\Hs}=\alpha^{-1}\|\eta-\eta_\star\|_{\Hd} \le\alpha^{-1}\sqrt{2\alpha g(\eta)}$. If $g(\eta)=0$ the left inequality gives $\eta=\eta_\star$; conversely $g(\eta_\star)=0$ because $\eta_\star$ minimizes $\pair{c_\star}{\cdot}$ over $\M$.

Thus $g(\eta_k)<\varepsilon$ is a stopping rule, and it bounds the error both in the objective and in the recovered cost.

The quantities controlled by (33) are measures and costs, not policies: the oracle may return a different optimal policy at each step, and (33) says nothing about them. The apprentice policy is therefore extracted once, at the end: when $c_k$ is accepted, any relaxed control that is optimal for $c_k$ is taken. For the exact solution this is consistent with Theorem 3.6, by which $\eta_{A,\star}$ is the occupation measure of a control that is optimal for $c_\star$.
